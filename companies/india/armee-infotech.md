## Executive Summary

ArMee Infotech is an IT infrastructure and managed-services company whose historical business is heavily oriented toward Government/PSU projects. It has recently expanded into solar EPC, solar PPAs and battery energy storage systems (BESS). The company listed on NSE/BSE on 30 September 2026 after a ₹300 crore fresh issue.

The important development is the change in the forward business mix. FY26 revenue was still dominated by IT infrastructure (85.81%), with renewable energy contributing 8.93%. However, the June 2026 order book was heavily renewable-oriented: third-party summaries of the filed prospectus report a consolidated order book of about ₹3,287 crore, while the standalone order book was ₹2,663.44 crore, of which ₹2,354.04 crore (88.38%) was renewable. The company therefore has the potential to become materially different from the historical IT-infrastructure integrator.

The strict 100X mathematics is more feasible than the preliminary assessment suggested. At approximately ₹300/share and roughly ₹937 crore market capitalisation on 1 October 2026, a 100X outcome implies about ₹93,700 crore of equity value. At a 25x terminal P/E and 15% net margin, this requires roughly ₹25,000 crore revenue. That is about 18x FY26 revenue and requires approximately 16% revenue CAGR over 20 years. With 20% dilution, the required revenue rises to about ₹31,700 crore, requiring approximately 17% CAGR over 20 years. These are demanding but not mathematically extraordinary assumptions.

The problem is not the TAM or the arithmetic. It is **economic quality**. FY26 PAT was ₹45.47 crore on ₹1,396.63 crore revenue, a 3.26% PAT margin. Trade receivables were about ₹541.8 crore, roughly 142 days of revenue, and unbilled revenue/contract assets were also material. FY25 operating cash flow was negative ₹17.98 crore and FY26 operating cash flow was only ₹12.48 crore. Borrowings rose materially, with FY26 debt/equity around 0.96x and interest cover around 3.45x. These characteristics make growth capital-intensive and weaken the link between revenue growth and per-share value creation.

**Classification: WATCH.** ArMee is a legitimate long-duration transformation candidate, but not CORE. The decisive research question is whether the renewable/BESS pivot can produce materially better margins, cash conversion and incremental ROIC than the legacy IT project/integration business.

No compelling investment case outside the strict 100X framework has been established at this stage; therefore **beyond_100x remains false**.

## 1. Thesis

### Core thesis

ArMee is attempting to use an established project-execution and Government/PSU contracting base to expand into a much larger renewable-energy opportunity.

The potential compounding path is:

Government/PSU execution capability
→ larger project credentials
→ greater PBG capacity
→ larger IT and energy contracts
→ renewable EPC
→ solar PPA / owned-project exposure
→ BESS
→ larger recurring/contracted energy cash flows
→ improved margins and ROIC
→ higher equity value per share.

The critical question is whether this is a genuine economic transformation or simply a shift from one project-based, working-capital-intensive business to another.

### What must be true

1. Renewable EPC/PPA/BESS projects execute at attractive gross and operating margins.
2. Renewable growth does not require proportionally larger working capital or debt.
3. PPA/BESS assets generate attractive returns after financing and maintenance costs.
4. Government/PSU concentration declines over time.
5. Customer concentration declines.
6. Receivable days normalise materially.
7. Operating cash flow consistently exceeds PAT over a cycle.
8. ROCE remains comfortably above the cost of capital as the company scales.
9. The company can fund growth without repeated material equity dilution.
10. Management demonstrates that renewable expertise is becoming an organisational capability rather than a new-project opportunity.

## 2. Business and Operating Trajectory

FY26 consolidated revenue from operations was ₹1,396.63 crore versus ₹1,313.31 crore in FY25 and ₹1,020.57 crore in FY24. FY26 EBITDA/operating profit was approximately ₹75.64 crore and PAT ₹45.47 crore. Revenue CAGR from FY24 to FY26 was about 17%, but FY26 revenue growth slowed to about 6.3%. FY25 profitability deteriorated before recovering in FY26.

FY26 revenue mix:
- IT infrastructure: 85.81%
- IT managed services: 5.26%
- Renewable energy: 8.93%

The renewable segment had no meaningful historical revenue base in FY24/FY25 and generated about ₹124.70 crore in FY26.

As of 30 June 2026, the company reported 99 ongoing projects:
- 65 IT infrastructure
- 21 IT managed services
- 10 renewable EPC
- 1 renewable PPA
- 2 BESS

The company also had 464 projects in warranty periods, generally three to five years.

The June 2026 standalone order book was approximately ₹2,663.44 crore, including:
- Solar EPC: ₹1,377.26 crore
- PPA: ₹381.60 crore
- BESS: ₹595.18 crore
- remaining IT/managed services.

This means approximately 88.38% of the standalone order book was renewable, even though renewable generated only 8.93% of FY26 revenue.

**Interpretation:** FACT for reported revenue/order-book mix. INFERENCE: the company is undergoing a significant business-mix transition.

## 3. TAM / Runway

India's renewable and electricity-storage markets provide a large structural runway. ArMee's DRHP describes India's solar capacity growth and national renewable targets, but the relevant investment question is not whether the industry TAM is large—it clearly is. The question is what share ArMee can capture at acceptable returns.

The company has stated a target of establishing cumulative solar capacity of 1GW by 2026. This should be treated as a MANAGEMENT CLAIM/target, not as evidence that 1GW has been economically deployed.

For the IT business, Government digitisation, education technology, public-distribution infrastructure and other public-sector digital projects provide continued demand, but the addressable market is competitive and tender-driven.

The more interesting runway is therefore the combination of:
- solar EPC;
- solar PPA/asset ownership;
- BESS;
- Government/PSU energy and digital infrastructure.

**Key limitation:** a large TAM does not establish a moat or high ROIC.

## 4. Moat

### Government/PSU execution track record — moderate

ArMee completed 116 Government/PSU projects in FY26, compared with 97 in FY25 and 62 in FY24. This creates qualification history and execution references.

However, government procurement is competitive and tender-based. The DRHP explicitly identifies prequalification, tender availability and aggressive price bidding as constraints.

### Procurement/vendor relationships — weak to moderate

The company works with technology partners for hardware/software. This creates execution capability but is not a proprietary technology moat.

### Project execution — moderate

Multi-location execution, technical manpower, procurement and project-management experience can create some operational advantage, but competitors can replicate these capabilities.

### Renewable capability — unproven

This is the key issue. Renewable is currently the largest part of the forward order book but has only recently become material in reported revenue. Management has limited historical operating evidence in this segment.

**Overall moat: 5/10.**

The current moat is execution-based rather than structural. A stronger moat could emerge if ArMee builds proprietary project-development capability, long-term PPAs, BESS operating expertise and repeat Government/PSU relationships at attractive returns.

## 5. Management and Governance

The company is promoter-controlled, with post-IPO promoter/promoter-group ownership around 69.35%. The IPO was entirely fresh equity rather than an OFS, so capital was raised into the company.

IPO proceeds were primarily earmarked for:
- ₹155 crore for performance bank guarantees/business expansion;
- ₹60 crore working capital;
- approximately ₹6.5 crore debt repayment;
- balance for general corporate purposes.

This use of proceeds highlights an important feature of the business: **growth requires financial capacity to support guarantees and working capital.**

The DRHP also discloses related-party transactions and loans from directors. These do not by themselves establish a governance failure, but they should remain part of the monitoring checklist.

**Management assessment: 5.5/10.**

The long-term question is capital allocation discipline after listing, particularly around renewable expansion and balance-sheet leverage.

### Working capital

This is a major framework concern because exceptional shareholder returns require the company to convert growth into distributable cash.

The combination of:

**Financial quality assessment: 4/10.**

## 10. Scenarios

### Bear

ArMee remains a low-margin project/integration business. Renewable EPC grows revenue but does not improve returns. Receivables remain high, debt increases and equity dilution funds growth.

**Outcome:** significant revenue growth but weak per-share compounding.

### Base

Revenue compounds in the low-to-mid teens. Renewable/BESS becomes a meaningful business. PAT margin rises toward 6–8%, operating cash flow becomes consistently positive and leverage stabilises.

**Outcome:** credible long-term wealth creation, but 100X remains uncertain.

### Bull

ArMee successfully transforms into an integrated digital + energy infrastructure platform. Renewable/PPA/BESS businesses produce superior margins and recurring cash flows. Government relationships translate into larger multi-state contracts, customer concentration declines and ROIC remains high.

**Outcome:** 50X becomes plausible and 100X becomes mathematically credible over a 20–25+ year period.

### 100X

Requires sustained mid-teens revenue CAGR for two decades, substantial margin expansion toward 12–15%+, strong FCF conversion, high incremental ROIC and limited dilution.

**Assessment:** possible, but low probability today.

## 11. Key Risks / Disconfirming Evidence

1. **Working-capital risk:** receivables and unbilled revenue can consume most of the accounting profit.
2. **Leverage risk:** debt/equity reached approximately 0.96x and interest cover declined.
3. **Customer concentration:** Government/PSU-related clients represented 83.84% of FY26 revenue; top five customers represented roughly 76.7%.
4. **Geographic concentration:** more than 86% of FY26 revenue came from Gujarat, Maharashtra and Tamil Nadu.
5. **Tender risk:** bid success rates have declined, according to prospectus-derived analysis.
6. **Renewable execution risk:** renewable is new relative to the historical IT business.
7. **BESS economics:** project-level economics and long-term utilisation must be demonstrated.
8. **Technology/vendor dependence:** hardware/software businesses rely on technology partners.
9. **Dilution:** future capital requirements could reduce per-share returns.
10. **Margin risk:** larger revenue does not guarantee higher margins.
11. **Governance/RPT risk:** related-party dealings and director loans require continued monitoring.
12. **Order-book risk:** order book is visibility, not guaranteed future revenue; contracts can be delayed, renegotiated or cancelled.

## 12. Sector / Bias Check

ArMee is useful for the 100X universe because it combines two areas already represented in the universe—technology infrastructure and energy transition—but with a different economic model.

It should not receive a premium merely because renewable energy is a large structural theme.

The comparison set should include:
- IT infrastructure / digital infrastructure companies;
- renewable EPC/developers;
- BESS/energy-storage businesses;
- Government infrastructure execution companies.

The key question is **relative incremental ROIC and cash conversion**, not thematic exposure.

## 13. Verdict

**WATCH — UNDER REVIEW**

ArMee qualifies for continued monitoring because:

- the starting market cap is small enough for 50–100X mathematics to remain feasible;
- revenue has grown materially;
- the order book is large relative to current revenue;
- renewable/BESS creates a potentially transformational new runway;
- the IPO has strengthened PBG/working-capital capacity.

It does **not** qualify for CORE because the most important elements of the thesis remain unproven:

- renewable project economics;
- sustainable margin expansion;
- FCF conversion;
- incremental ROIC;
- balance-sheet discipline;
- customer concentration reduction;
- durable moat.

**Beyond 100X: false.** The research has not yet established a sufficiently compelling alternative case outside the strict exceptional-return framework to justify setting this flag true.
