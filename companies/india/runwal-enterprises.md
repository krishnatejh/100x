## Executive Summary

Runwal is a Mumbai-focused real-estate developer with 19 completed projects, 28 ongoing projects and 33 upcoming projects as of March 31, 2026, covering 88.37 million sq ft of developable/estimated developable area. FY26 revenue was ₹1,798.9 Cr and PAT ₹185.8 Cr, but operating cash flow remained negative at approximately ₹180.7 Cr. FY26 inventory was ~₹8,451 Cr and total debt ~₹2,909 Cr.

Classification: WATCH. The pipeline and Mumbai positioning create meaningful business potential, but leverage, inventory, negative cash conversion and the enormous scale required for 100X keep probability of success low.

## 100X Mathematics

At ₹305 IPO price, post-issue equity value is ~₹4,508 Cr. A 100X outcome is ~₹4.51 lakh Cr equity value. At 25x terminal P/E and 15% net margin, required PAT is ~₹18,032 Cr and revenue ~₹1.20 lakh Cr, about 67x FY26 revenue and requiring ~23.1% revenue CAGR for 20 years. At 10% margin, required revenue is ~₹1.80 lakh Cr and ~25.1% CAGR.

## Thesis

Potential path: Mumbai brand/project execution → pipeline → bookings/collections → completion → inventory monetisation → lower leverage → greater JDA/JV/asset-light growth → higher ROE and free cash generation.

The key test is whether Runwal can become a high-ROIC, cash-generative and increasingly asset-light development platform rather than simply a larger leveraged developer.

## Risks

Capital intensity; leverage; persistent negative CFO; inventory; Mumbai concentration; project/approval delays; real-estate cyclicality; competition; JDA economics; future dilution; governance/capital allocation; and the very large absolute scale required for 100X.

## Review Triggers

Upgrade if bookings compound >15–20%, CFO becomes consistently positive, inventory turns improve, debt/equity falls, ROE/ROIC stays >20% without rising leverage, asset-light/JDA growth becomes meaningful, project delays decline and dilution remains disciplined.

Downgrade if debt rapidly rebuilds, CFO stays deeply negative despite rising PAT, inventory grows faster than bookings, delays increase, bookings stagnate or new acquisitions require repeated equity.
