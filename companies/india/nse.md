## Executive Thesis

NSE is a critical Indian market-infrastructure business with potential structural advantages from liquidity, network effects, technology, ecosystem depth, and operating leverage. However, its investment case is materially affected by revenue concentration in transaction charges and options activity, regulatory intervention, trading-volume cyclicality, and a demanding IPO valuation.

The company merits continued research as a potential long-term financial-infrastructure compounder, but current evidence does not establish a credible 50X–100X per-share pathway from the IPO valuation.

## Business and Moat

- Strong market position and liquidity ecosystem.
- Potential network effects: liquidity can attract participants and reinforce liquidity.
- Technology, clearing, index, data, and listing-related capabilities may create operating leverage and switching friction.
- Competitive position must be tested against BSE and possible regulatory/product changes.

## Financial and Valuation Observations

The September 2026 IPO price band was reported at ₹1,700–₹1,785 per share, with an issue size of approximately ₹22,561 crore and an offer-for-sale structure. Reported valuation at the upper band was approximately ₹4.42 lakh crore.

Reported FY26 figures used in preliminary analysis included operating revenue of approximately ₹16,601 crore and PAT of approximately ₹10,302 crore. These figures imply a rough trailing P/E near 43x using the reported valuation, subject to verification against the final RHP, diluted share count, and accounting presentation.

The IPO is an offer for sale; therefore, the proceeds primarily go to selling shareholders rather than directly funding NSE's expansion.

## Revenue Quality and Risks

- Transaction charges represent a substantial share of operating revenue.
- Options activity is a major contributor and is sensitive to regulation, taxation, product design, and trading behavior.
- Regulatory changes can materially affect volumes, pricing, and profitability.
- Revenue concentration among major trading members requires monitoring.
- Technology, cyber, operational, legal, and reputational risks are material.
- Valuation compression could reduce shareholder returns even if the business continues to grow.

## 50X / 100X Mathematics

Using an illustrative market capitalization of ₹4.42 lakh crore:

- 10X: ₹44.2 lakh crore
- 50X: ₹221 lakh crore
- 100X: ₹442 lakh crore

A 100X market-cap outcome would require approximately 100X earnings if the valuation multiple remained unchanged. If the P/E multiple compressed from approximately 43x to 25x, earnings would need to increase by roughly 172x for a 100X market-cap outcome. This is a mathematical sensitivity, not a forecast.

The final assessment must incorporate per-share outcomes, dividends, buybacks, retained capital, and any changes in share count.

## Scenario Framing

### Bear

Structural decline or stagnation in derivatives-related activity, adverse regulatory changes, weak diversification, and earnings compression.

### Base

Long-term growth in Indian capital markets, continued leadership, gradual diversification, and earnings compounding below exceptional-return requirements.

### Bull

Sustained expansion in Indian capital-market participation, durable leadership, successful growth in data/index/listing/new-product businesses, and strong long-term earnings compounding.

A bull business-growth case does not automatically establish a 100X shareholder-return case from the IPO valuation.
