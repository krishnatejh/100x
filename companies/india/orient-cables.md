## Executive Summary

Orient Cables (India) is a B2B cable manufacturer focused primarily on networking cables and passive networking products, with a growing specialty power and optical-fibre business. It serves telecom, broadband, data centres, renewable energy, smart buildings, security, system integration, FMEG and automotive customers. The company was incorporated in 2005 and operates two Bhiwadi facilities and a Bengaluru facility that commenced commercial operations in May 2026.

The investment case is more interesting than a generic cable-manufacturer thesis. The company reports approximately 22.9% share of India's networking-cable market in FY26 according to the 1Lattice industry report commissioned for the offer document, up from approximately 16% in FY22. Revenue from operations grew from ₹657.8 crore in FY24 to ₹1,171.7 crore in FY26, a roughly 33.5% two-year CAGR. Specialty power and optical-fibre revenue increased sharply to ₹250.4 crore in FY26 and represented 21.4% of revenue, versus 12% in FY25. In Q1 FY27, this mix increased further to 31.2%.

However, the quality of that growth is not yet proven. FY26 PAT was approximately ₹53.6 crore, essentially flat versus FY25 despite 42% revenue growth. EBITDA margin fell from 10.17% in FY25 to 8.23% in FY26. Operating cash flow was negative ₹26.6 crore in FY26 after negative ₹9.8 crore in FY25, versus positive ₹42.2 crore in FY24. Net working-capital days increased to 48 in FY26 from 27 in FY25, while net debt/equity reached approximately 0.94x.

The IPO price of ₹272 implies approximately ₹3,095 crore post-issue equity value. At this starting point, a 100X outcome requires approximately ₹3.10 lakh crore equity value. The arithmetic is possible only with a major combination of sustained high revenue growth and improved margins. At 25x terminal P/E and 15% net margin, a 100X outcome requires approximately ₹82,500 crore revenue—about 70x FY26 revenue—and roughly 23% revenue CAGR over 20 years. A more optimistic 20x P/E / 20% margin case still requires about ₹77,400 crore revenue. Therefore the mathematical hurdle is substantially higher than for ArMee.

The key potential catalyst is not merely cable-volume growth. It is whether Orient can evolve from a networking-cable manufacturer into a broader, higher-value connectivity and specialty-cable platform benefiting from data centres, fibre, electrification, renewable energy and EV infrastructure, while sustaining ROCE above 20% and converting earnings into cash.

**Classification: WATCH.** The company has enough business quality and runway to justify long-term monitoring, but the evidence is insufficient for CORE. The principal disconfirming evidence is weak FY26 cash conversion, rising working capital, customer concentration, margin compression and the high valuation relative to current earnings.

No compelling investment case outside the strict 100X framework has been established at this stage; therefore **beyond_100x remains false**.

## 1. Thesis

### Core thesis

Orient Cables could compound if it uses its established networking-cable position and customer relationships to move progressively toward higher-value specialty connectivity products.

Potential path:

Networking cables
→ scale and customer qualification
→ optical fibre / specialty power
→ data-centre connectivity
→ renewable and electrification applications
→ EV and specialty cable products
→ broader engineered connectivity platform
→ higher mix of differentiated products
→ higher margins and ROCE
→ stronger free cash flow
→ long-duration compounding.

The central question is whether the company can achieve this mix shift without turning growth into proportionally higher working-capital and debt requirements.

### What must be true

1. Networking-cable market growth remains structurally high enough to support sustained volume growth.
2. Orient maintains or increases its market share without sacrificing margins.
3. Specialty power and optical-fibre products become a materially larger share of revenue.
4. New products such as E-Beam specialty cables, solar junction boxes, EV cable assemblies and harnesses achieve meaningful commercial scale.
5. EBITDA margin recovers from the FY26 decline and eventually reaches double digits sustainably.
6. PAT margin can move toward 10–15% over the long term.
7. ROCE remains above 20% through expansion rather than being temporarily inflated by a small capital base.
8. Working-capital intensity normalises and operating cash flow consistently exceeds PAT over a cycle.
9. Customer concentration falls as the company adds customers and geographies.
10. Debt declines relative to EBITDA after IPO proceeds and capacity expansion.
11. The Orient brand dispute does not materially impair customer relationships or branding.
12. Growth can be funded without repeated equity dilution.

## 3. TAM / Runway

The prospectus cites a commissioned 1Lattice report estimating India's networking-cables market at approximately ₹35.9 billion in FY26 and projecting approximately ₹84.5 billion by FY31, implying roughly 18.7% CAGR. The earlier DRHP described FY25 networking-cable market size of approximately ₹29.2 billion and FY25–FY30 projected CAGR of approximately 19.7%.

The same industry research describes the Indian fibre-optic-cables market as approximately ₹105 billion in FY26, with strong projected growth.

Data-centre infrastructure is another structural demand driver. The prospectus notes Indian data-centre power capacity grew from about 0.3 GW in FY19 to about 1.3 GW in FY25 and projects approximately 4.7–5.7 GW by FY30. Cables are a necessary component of both network and power infrastructure.

These TAM figures are **industry-source estimates commissioned by the company**, not independent forecasts. They establish runway, not Orient's eventual market share or returns.

### Market-share position

The 1Lattice report places Orient among India's top four networking-cable players with approximately 22.9% FY26 market share, up from approximately 16% in FY22.

This is one of the stronger pieces of evidence in the thesis because it suggests Orient has already achieved meaningful scale rather than being an unproven entrant.

**Moat implication:** market position is meaningful but not necessarily durable. Cable manufacturing has material raw-material exposure and competition from larger, better-capitalised players.

## 4. Moat

### Market position — moderate

A reported ~22.9% networking-cable market share provides scale, customer references and procurement credibility.

### Customer relationships — moderate

The company reports that repeat customers among its top 50 customers generated approximately 93.9% of FY26 revenue, and its top ten customer relationships averaged more than nine years. It also supplies two of India's three largest telecom companies by revenue.

This indicates substantial customer stickiness, but concentration remains high.

### Manufacturing / execution — moderate

Three facilities and approximately 895,776 km annualised cable capacity as of June 30, 2026 provide scale. Manufacturing to customer specifications and qualification with large customers can create switching friction.

### Technology / IP — weak to moderate

The current portfolio is primarily established cable technology. Newer products could create differentiation, but there is insufficient evidence yet of proprietary technology or pricing power.

### Cost advantage — unproven

Copper and polymer inputs are significant. The company states that it generally passes raw-material cost movements through to customers, but the FY26 margin decline shows that pass-through does not eliminate all economic pressure.

**Overall moat: 6/10.**

The moat is primarily scale + customer qualification + repeat relationships rather than proprietary technology.

## 5. Management and Capital Allocation

The company is promoter-controlled. Post-IPO promoter ownership is approximately 82.17%.

The IPO consists of:
- ₹320 crore fresh issue
- ₹232 crore OFS

The fresh issue is intended primarily for:
- approximately ₹155.5 crore repayment/prepayment of debt;
- approximately ₹91.5 crore manufacturing capex;
- balance toward other stated corporate purposes.

The capital structure therefore improves after the IPO if debt repayment is executed as disclosed.

The promoters are monetising part of their ownership through the OFS. This is not automatically negative, but it means the IPO is not solely a growth-capital raise.

### Governance / legal watchpoint

There is an ongoing dispute concerning use of the “ORIENT” brand involving Orient Electric. The company's November 2025 corrigendum/addendum disclosed civil/IP-related proceedings. The prospectus also disclosed litigation involving promoters/directors, although it did not disclose outstanding material civil litigation against the company in the DRHP sections reviewed.

The brand dispute should remain on the monitoring list because brand confusion could affect customer relationships and commercial positioning even if the ultimate legal outcome is uncertain.

**Management / governance assessment: 6/10**, with the score constrained by limited listed-company track record and the need to observe post-IPO capital allocation.

Result: Orient remains a competent cable manufacturer but does not develop the economics required for exceptional long-term shareholder returns.

Result: credible long-term compounder, but still not automatically a 100X candidate from the IPO valuation.

- Revenue compounds >20% for 15–20+ years.

- Specialty and engineered products become a major portion of revenue.

Only under a combination like this does the 100X mathematics become credible.

## 9. Key Risks / Why It Might NOT Become 100X

1. **Commodity exposure:** copper and polymer costs can compress margins.
2. **Customer concentration:** top customers account for a large portion of revenue.
3. **Margin compression:** FY26 demonstrates that high revenue growth does not guarantee earnings growth.
4. **Cash conversion:** negative OCF in FY25/FY26 is a major warning signal.
5. **Working capital:** NWC days rose sharply to 48.
6. **Leverage:** net debt/equity reached 0.94x before IPO debt repayment.
7. **Competition:** larger cable players have greater scale and distribution.
8. **Technology transition:** the networking-cable market can evolve toward newer standards/products.
9. **New-product execution:** E-Beam, EV, solar and drone-related products remain early-stage.
10. **Customer concentration:** loss or renegotiation of a major customer could materially affect results.
11. **Brand litigation:** the Orient name dispute could create commercial and legal uncertainty.
12. **Valuation:** ~57x FY26 earnings at the IPO price leaves little room for mediocre execution.
13. **Capital intensity:** capacity expansion can consume cash before producing returns.
14. **Dilution:** future expansion may require additional equity if internal cash generation remains weak.

## 10. Sector / Bias Check

Orient is useful for the 100X universe because it provides exposure to a different part of the electrification/data-infrastructure chain.

The existing universe already contains:
- Dynamic Cables — electrical equipment / electrification / grid modernization
- Pitti Engineering — electrical equipment / electrification / precision manufacturing
- Quality Power — electrical equipment / grid modernization / electrification

Orient should therefore be tested against these candidates rather than retained simply because it is a new IPO.

The strongest differentiator is Orient's combination of networking/data cables with specialty power and optical fibre, giving it more exposure to data-centre and digital-infrastructure growth than a conventional grid-cable company.

However, the framework should apply a higher evidence bar to the electrical-equipment cluster because multiple candidates already occupy it.

## 12. Review Triggers

### Upgrade triggers

- FY27/FY28 EBITDA margin sustainably >10–11%.
- PAT growth begins materially exceeding revenue growth.
- Specialty/fibre mix exceeds 35–40% with evidence of superior economics.
- OCF becomes consistently positive and exceeds PAT.
- NWC days return toward <30.
- ROCE sustains >20–25%.
- Debt/equity falls materially after IPO.
- New products become >5–10% of revenue with attractive margins.
- Customer concentration falls.
- International exports scale meaningfully.
- Evidence of pricing power or differentiated qualification emerges.

### Downgrade triggers

- EBITDA margin remains <8% despite scale.
- OCF remains negative for another full year.
- NWC days continue rising.
- Debt rebuilds rapidly after IPO repayment.
- Major customer concentration worsens.
- Specialty products fail to scale.
- Material adverse development in brand litigation.
- Significant equity dilution is required to fund ordinary growth.

## 13. Verdict

**WATCH — not CORE.**

Orient Cables is a credible small/mid-cap growth story with a meaningful existing networking-cable position, strong historical ROCE and a potentially attractive specialty/fibre/data-centre/electrification runway.

But the current evidence does not support a high-conviction 100X classification.

The most important issue is not whether the company can grow revenue. It already has.

The question is whether it can convert that growth into **higher-quality earnings and cash flow**.

The IPO valuation makes this especially important: at approximately ₹3,095 crore equity value and ~57.5x FY26 earnings, the market is already assigning significant value to future growth. For a 100X outcome, Orient would need approximately 20%+ revenue CAGR for two decades plus substantial margin expansion.

Therefore the correct framework stance is:

**Own/track if already allotted; do not use the IPO allotment as a reason to add capital. Let the business prove the economics first.**

This is not a short-term listing call. The decisive evidence should come from FY27–FY29 operating performance.
