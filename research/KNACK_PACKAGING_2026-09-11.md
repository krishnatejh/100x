# Knack Packaging — 100X Framework Review

**Review date:** 2026-09-11  
**Verdict:** REJECTED for the dedicated 100X corpus  
**Position action:** EXIT  
**Fresh capital action:** WAIT

## Executive conclusion

Knack Packaging is a financially strong, fast-growing specialised packaging manufacturer, but it does not clear the project's probability-weighted 100X hurdle. The principal problem is not near-term execution: reported growth, margins and returns on capital are attractive. The problem is the extreme scale required from the current market value and the absence of sufficiently strong evidence for a durable, scalable moat and reinvestment runway capable of supporting that scale.

## FACTS

- FY23–FY26 consolidated revenue grew from about ₹518 crore to ₹844 crore; PAT grew from about ₹20 crore to ₹93 crore. FY26 EBITDA was about ₹172 crore and reported EBITDA margin about 20.4%.
- FY26 reported RoCE was about 46.7%, RoE about 35.8%, and debt/equity about 0.62x.
- Q1 FY27 consolidated revenue was about ₹262.5 crore, +41% YoY; PAT was about ₹30.5 crore, +48% YoY; EBITDA margin was about 22.4%.
- Current price around 10–11 September 2026 was approximately ₹187, implying market capitalisation around ₹2,231 crore.
- The IPO raised ₹380 crore through fresh equity; management indicated approximately ₹320 crore for expansion. Capacity is targeted to rise to roughly 70,000 MTPA by October 2027.
- Customer and supplier concentration is meaningful. The largest customer represented about 16.7% of FY26 revenue; the top 10 customers about 40.9%. The largest supplier represented about 35.8% of raw-material purchases.

## 100X mathematics

At roughly ₹2,231 crore market capitalisation:
- 10X = ₹22,310 crore
- 50X = ₹111,550 crore
- 100X = ₹223,100 crore (₹2.23 lakh crore)

A 100X outcome in 20 years requires approximately 25.9% CAGR; in 25 years approximately 20.2% CAGR.

Even if the company ultimately sustained a high 15% net margin, a ₹2.23 lakh crore valuation would require enormous future earnings. At a 25x terminal P/E, net profit would need to approach ₹8,900 crore and revenue roughly ₹59,000 crore at a 15% net margin. At a 30x P/E, profit would still be about ₹7,400 crore and revenue about ₹49,500 crore.

These are not impossible mathematical outcomes, but they require Knack to evolve from an approximately ₹844 crore revenue specialised packaging manufacturer into a business tens of thousands of crores in revenue with unusually high and durable margins. Current evidence does not establish that path.

## What is attractive

1. Strong historical growth and accelerating Q1 FY27 performance.
2. High reported RoCE/ROE.
3. Integrated manufacturing and specialised product mix.
4. Export exposure and a broad product/customer base.
5. Premium pinch-bottom bags and capacity expansion could support several years of growth.

## What breaks the 100X thesis

### 1. TAM and scale mismatch
Packaging demand is large, but a large industry is not enough. The company needs an extraordinary share capture and/or a fundamental business-model evolution.

### 2. Moat evidence is insufficient
Integration, process know-how, customer relationships and product customisation are positives, but the evidence for software-like switching costs, network effects, unique IP, regulatory exclusivity or extraordinary pricing power is limited.

### 3. Manufacturing economics
Growth requires capacity, equipment, working capital and execution. This can produce excellent returns, but it is structurally harder to compound at extreme rates for decades than a highly scalable asset-light platform.

### 4. Concentration and commodity exposure
Customer concentration and polypropylene/raw-material exposure can affect margins and bargaining power. Pass-through mechanisms reduce but do not eliminate the risk.

### 5. Valuation leaves less room for error
At approximately ₹2,231 crore market capitalisation and around 25x TTM P/E, the company is no longer a tiny undiscovered business. Excellent growth is already visible and partially reflected in the valuation.

## Scenarios

**Bear:** capacity expansion faces slower demand or margin normalisation; commodity/customer risks reduce returns. Good business but ordinary shareholder returns.

**Base:** successful expansion, sustained 15–20%+ growth for a number of years, strong returns on capital. Could become a materially larger packaging company and potentially be a good investment, but not a credible 100X outcome from the current base.

**Bull:** premium products, exports and capacity expansion sustain high growth and margins for a prolonged period. Significant multi-bagger potential remains possible.

**100X:** would require decades of exceptional growth, major market-share gains, sustained high returns, and likely expansion well beyond the current packaging niche. This is possible only under a chain of aggressive assumptions; probability is too low for scarce 100X corpus capital.

## Scores

- Business Potential: **5.5/10**
- Probability of Success: **6.0/10**
- Investment Attractiveness: **4.5/10**

## Final decision

**REJECTED for the 100X corpus.**

This is not a judgement that Knack Packaging is a bad company. It is a capital-allocation judgement: when competing globally for a small number of exceptional long-duration opportunities, Knack does not currently rank high enough.

**Action for an existing leftover IPO position: EXIT rather than continue holding solely because it was allotted in the IPO. Redeployment into the 100X corpus is reasonable, subject to portfolio/tax/liquidity considerations.**
